## 1. Introduction

During my internship, I had the opportunity to work with **PIVOT**, a Quebec-based company developing an employee management application for restaurants. Their mission is to simplify operations through a centralized digital tool, focused on scheduling, communication, and staff organization.

As a developer intern, I was primarily involved in **front-end development**, contributing to UI improvements, fixing bugs, and occasionally working on **back-end** adjustments. The experience was hands-on, practical, and had a real impact on the product.

---

## 2. Description of the Internship

### 🛠️ Onboarding & Workflow

From day one, I was welcomed by a senior developer who served as my mentor. He guided me through setting up the development environment, understanding the project structure, and adapting to the team's workflow. I was operational and contributing by the end of the first day. I was always encouraged to ask questions when I got stuck. Even after my mentor returned to France, we continued to have regular video calls where I could ask for guidance, receive feedback, and accelerate my learning.

### 💻 My Tasks

Most of my work focused on the **front-end**, using technologies like:

- **React** for building user interfaces
- **Redux** for state management
- **React Native** for mobile components
- Occasional updates to the **Firebase** back-end

My responsibilities included taking on feature and bug tickets, investigating the codebase, proposing solutions, and submitting pull requests for review. One of the company’s developers also mentored me by reviewing my code and helping me write better, more efficient components.

I also assisted some of my colleagues with IT-related issues — whether it was troubleshooting Excel, Word, or general computer problems.

## 3. New Learnings

This internship helped me grow both technically and personally. Here’s what I gained:

### 🧠 Technical Learnings

- Deepened my understanding of **React** and **Redux** through real-world development
- Used **React Native** in a production environment for the first time
- Gained practical experience with **Firebase** and back-end integration
- Learned how to collaborate effectively using **Git**, **Slack**, and **Jira**

This was my first serious experience with React. Before the internship, I had followed a tutorial as part of the interview process to learn the basics. But it was at PIVOT that I truly **deepened my understanding** of the technology.

My mentor encouraged me to go beyond the requirements of the project and to explore more. He introduced me to useful tools and technologies for full-stack development like **Supabase** and **Figma**, pushing me to think bigger and learn more.

## 4. Commentary

### 🌱 i. Benefits of the Internship

This internship was a **game-changer**. It gave me a real-world glimpse into life as a developer — contributing to a live product, working in a team, and writing code that actually gets used.

The biggest benefit? **Confidence**. I now feel more capable, more comfortable in a development environment, and more prepared for future roles in tech. I don’t just write code — I think like a developer. I understand my strengths, and I see clearly where I can improve.

### ⚙️ ii. Skills

**Skills from the Computer Science program I applied:**

- Understanding of the software development lifecycle
- Ability to structure clean, scalable front-end architecture
- Effective use of Git for version control and collaboration
- Reading and writing production-level code

**Skills I brought from personal experience:**

- Quick self-learning and initiative
- Clear written communication within the team
- Natural curiosity and love for clean, functional design

**Skills I lacked or want to improve:**

- Stronger knowledge of back-end systems and API design

### 💬 iii. Final Thoughts

At first, I wanted to impress — to be perfect. But this internship taught me that **software development is not a sprint, it's a journey**. It's about showing up every day, getting a little better, and building things that matter.

You grow by doing. By trying. By failing sometimes.  
By putting in the work — during work hours and after — to become a better version of yourself.
